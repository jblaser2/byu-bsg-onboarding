# FAQ

The questions every new member asks, answered honestly.

## "I have no idea what I'm supposed to be doing. Is that normal?"

Yes, very. It's normal enough that this entire website exists because of it. Read
[About Me](about-me.md) — I felt lost for months when I started. The hands-off culture means
nobody hands you a task list; your job is to ask questions and find ways to contribute. It feels
uncomfortable at first, and it tends to become one of the best parts.

**What to do right now:** set up a recurring meeting with Dr. Hart or a teammate (see
[Who's Who](contacts.md)), and start working through [Getting Started](getting-started.md).

## "Do I really have to work 10 hours a week?"

Yes — 10 hours is the standard baseline, and you can go up to an average of 20 hours a week. But
"work" is broad: reading about the field, learning a new programming language, meeting with
researchers, problem-solving, and writing up your work all count. What your days actually look
like is up to you. See [the 10-hour standard](index.md#group-culture).

## "How do I get on the supercomputer?"

It's one of several first steps, not the most important one. See
[Getting Started](getting-started.md) for the overview, and [The Supercomputer](supercomputer.md)
for the account request walkthrough.

## "I don't know what half these words mean."

That's what the [Glossary](glossary.md) is for. CryoET, STA, T4SS, tomogram, PI — all decoded
there.

## "Do I need to be a machine-learning or CryoEM expert before I'm useful?"

No. Build enough intuition to follow conversations and read the code, then learn the rest on a
real problem. Start with [Understanding CryoEM](cryoem.md) and
[Machine Learning](machine-learning.md) — skim first, master later.

## "What's Tea & Chat? Do I have to go?"

It's Dr. Hart's weekly meeting where the group drinks homemade tea and talks about life, things
we've learned, a shared book, and hard research problems. You should go — it's where you'll
actually get to know the group, and where a lot of good thinking happens.

## "Wait, there's handball?"

There is. Dr. Hart is president of the handball club and enjoys when students come play.

## "Do I have to publish a paper?"

You should aim to. Writing and submitting at least one article for publication while you're here
is a goal worth setting — it's how the field shares and builds on work, and it's good experience.

## "Should I go to conferences?"

Yes, whenever you get the chance. They're for finding collaborators, learning the state of the
art, and getting feedback. See [Conferences](conferences.md).

## "I'm reading and my brain hurts."

That's part of learning. Protect the priorities: relationships, health, then learning. Rest is
part of the work too.
